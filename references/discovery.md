# Discovery Playbook

The objective is broad market discovery, not lookalike matching.

## 1. Understand the target

Extract and cite:

- commercial offer, differentiators, customer types, and target markets;
- core product/service topics and adjacent problems the audience researches;
- useful target pages and claims that a publisher could cite naturally;
- languages, geography, and excluded categories.

Build topic clusters rather than one keyword list. For a steel-building supplier, for example, clusters may include industrial construction, warehouses and logistics, architecture, commercial property, building envelopes, roofing, energy efficiency, manufacturing facilities, cold storage, agriculture, aviation hangars, and sustainability.

## 2. Build a query matrix

Run multiple query families across core and adjacent clusters, localized for the campaign language and geography:

- industry publications: `<topic> magazine`, `<topic> news`, `<topic> publication`;
- contributions: `<topic> "write for us"`, `contribute`, `guest article`, `submit article`;
- commercial collaboration: `<topic> sponsored content`, `advertise`, `media kit`, `partnership`;
- resource opportunities: `<topic> resources`, `suppliers`, `tools`, `guide`, `roundup`, `directory`;
- editorial gaps: existing guides whose audience could benefit from the target's expertise, data, visual asset, or case study;
- organizations: associations, institutes, communities, newsletters, podcasts, and event publications;
- competitors: referring pages or publisher domains only when a backlink tool or public evidence is already accessible.

Do not let any single query family dominate the result. Keep the exact query and source type in the search log.

## 3. Capture raw candidates

For each unique domain, capture:

- site and exact evidence URL;
- discovery query and method;
- core or adjacent topic connection;
- country/region and language when verifiable;
- `explicit` or `editorial` pool;
- a concise reason it could create reader or citation value;
- public cooperation and price evidence, if present;
- last verified date and evidence freshness;
- risk signals and uncertainty.
- traffic estimate, provider, country database or scope, and check date when an authorized tool is available;
- candidate guest/contributor article URLs for indexation and link-attribute sampling.

Normalize the hostname before deduplication. A repeated historical domain remains eligible but must be flagged. An exact previously purchased placement page must not be proposed as new.

## 4. Research depth

Raw candidates need enough evidence to decide whether to evaluate further. Final candidates require direct inspection of the publication or the strongest accessible page. Search snippets alone are not sufficient for final cooperation claims or exact prices.

Use an accessible Ahrefs, Semrush, or equivalent account only when already authorized. Record the provider and observation date. Without it, rely on public search presence, publishing activity, content quality, authorship, discoverability, and indexability. Leave unavailable metrics blank.

Before final selection, run the traffic/indexation/link-attribute procedure in [quality-checks.md](quality-checks.md). A collaboration page is not a substitute for a published-article sample.

## 5. Selection balance

Evaluate at least 90 raw domains, then select up to 30 with evidence completeness, topical coverage, price/effect potential, and source diversity. Do not pad the shortlist. Avoid filling it with many near-identical sites from the same query, network, or category.
